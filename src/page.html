3D printing troubleshooting

Common FDM print problems and what usually fixes them. Not specific to any model here — this applies to prints from any slicer.

Warping & lifting corners

Looks like

  • Corners or edges curl up off the bed as printing continues

Likely causes

  • Bed too cold, or uneven heating across the bed
  • Drafts or AC/fan airflow cooling one side of the part faster
  • Material shrinks as it cools (ABS, PETG and nylon are worse than PLA)

Try

  • Raise bed temperature a few degrees and make sure it's fully preheated before starting
  • Use an enclosure or draft shield; close windows/doors near the printer
  • Add a brim or raft for more edge adhesion
  • Avoid sharp 90° corners in the design where possible; a fillet reduces warping stress
Poor first-layer adhesion

Looks like

  • Print detaches from the bed partway through, or the first layer looks patchy/stringy instead of smooth

Likely causes

  • Nozzle too far from (or too close to) the bed
  • Bed not level, or not clean (oils, dust)
  • Bed temperature too low for the material, or no adhesion aid used

Try

  • Re-level the bed and recalibrate the first-layer (Z-offset/live-leveling) height
  • Clean the bed with isopropyl alcohol between prints
  • Use a glue stick, hairspray, or PEI/textured sheet suited to the material
  • Slow down the first layer and increase first-layer line width slightly in the slicer
Stringing & oozing

Looks like

  • Thin hair-like strands of plastic between separate parts of the print, especially after travel moves

Likely causes

  • Nozzle temperature too high, so filament drips while traveling
  • Retraction distance/speed too low, or disabled
  • Filament has absorbed moisture

Try

  • Lower the nozzle temperature in 5°C steps until stringing clears up
  • Increase retraction distance and speed (direct-drive extruders need far less retraction than Bowden setups)
  • Turn on "combing"/avoid-crossing-perimeters travel options in the slicer
  • Dry the filament (a food dehydrator or filament dryer, 4–8h) if it's been exposed to humid air
Layer shifting

Looks like

  • Layers suddenly offset sideways partway up the print, like the top half slid over

Likely causes

  • Belts loose or skipping on a pulley
  • Print speed/acceleration too high for the printer, causing motors to skip steps
  • Something physically snagging the print head (a cable, a tall thin part, a wipe tower)

Try

  • Check belt tension and pulley set-screws on X and Y axes
  • Lower print speed and acceleration, especially for tall or detailed models
  • Make sure cables can move freely through their full range and nothing overhangs into the gantry's path
Under-extrusion (gaps, weak or missing lines)

Looks like

  • Visible gaps between lines, patchy top surfaces, or layers that look thin/starved of plastic

Likely causes

  • Partially clogged nozzle, or worn extruder gear not gripping filament
  • Flow rate or filament diameter set incorrectly in the slicer
  • Nozzle temperature too low for the material to flow properly

Try

  • Run a cold pull or needle-clean the nozzle; check the filament diameter with calipers against the slicer setting
  • Increase flow rate a few percent, or raise nozzle temperature slightly
  • Check the extruder idler tension — too loose and the gear slips, too tight and it can grind through the filament
Over-extrusion (blobs, rough or oversized surfaces)

Looks like

  • Bumpy, blobby surfaces; parts measuring slightly larger than designed; seams that bulge

Likely causes

  • Flow rate set too high
  • Filament diameter in the slicer doesn't match the actual filament
  • E-steps miscalibrated on the extruder

Try

  • Lower flow rate in small steps (try 95-98% first)
  • Measure the actual filament diameter and update the slicer setting
  • Run an extruder calibration (mark filament, extrude 100mm commanded, measure what actually fed through)
Elephant's foot (bulging first layers)

Looks like

  • The bottom few layers bulge outward slightly compared to the rest of the part, so it doesn't sit flush against mating parts

Likely causes

  • Bed too hot or nozzle too close, squashing the first layers under the weight of the part above

Try

  • Reduce bed temperature slightly, or the first-layer height/Z-offset
  • Enable "elephant foot compensation" in the slicer if it's available — it tapers the bottom layers inward slightly to cancel the bulge
Clogged or jammed nozzle

Looks like

  • Extruder motor clicking, no filament coming out, or filament grinding into a flat spot and no longer feeding

Likely causes

  • Debris or burnt (carbonized) filament stuck in the nozzle or heat-break
  • Printing too cool for the material, so it doesn't melt fully
  • Retraction settings too aggressive, pulling molten filament back into a cooler part of the hotend where it solidifies

Try

  • Do a cold pull: heat to printing temp, then let it cool a little and firmly pull the filament out to drag debris with it; repeat until it comes out clean
  • Use a cleaning filament or needle to clear the nozzle tip
  • Check that retraction distance isn't excessive for the hotend type (Bowden vs. direct-drive)
Weak layer bonding / parts snap apart along layer lines

Looks like

  • The part breaks cleanly along a layer boundary with little force, even though the walls look fine

Likely causes

  • Nozzle temperature too low for good layer fusion
  • Print cooling fan blowing too hard too early, chilling each layer before the next bonds to it
  • Print speed too fast for the material to fuse properly

Try

  • Raise nozzle temperature a little
  • Reduce part-cooling fan speed, especially for the first several layers
  • Slow down print speed, particularly on outer walls
Rough or gappy top surfaces (pillowing)

Looks like

  • The top of a flat area looks bubbly, has small holes, or sags between infill lines

Likely causes

  • Not enough top solid layers to fully bridge over the infill pattern
  • Cooling fan too weak on top layers, so each one sags before it sets
  • Infill density/pattern leaves gaps too wide to bridge cleanly

Try

  • Increase top solid layer count (often 4-6 for a 0.2mm layer height)
  • Increase cooling fan speed for top layers specifically
  • Raise infill density, or switch to a grid/gyroid pattern with tighter spacing
Z-banding / ringing (wavy surface texture)

Looks like

  • Regular horizontal wavy bands up the side of the print, or ghosting/echoes near sharp corners

Likely causes

  • Z-axis lead screw/rod wobble or a loose coupler
  • Print speed and acceleration too high, causing the frame to vibrate (ringing specifically)
  • Loose belts or a gantry that isn't rigid

Try

  • Check the Z-axis coupler and lead screw for play; add an anti-backlash nut if needed
  • Lower acceleration/jerk settings, or enable input shaping if the firmware supports it
  • Tighten belts and frame fasteners
Mesh errors in the slicer ("non-manifold", holes, inverted faces)

Looks like

  • The slicer flags the model with warnings about open surfaces or errors, or the preview shows missing faces

Likely causes

  • The STL has gaps, duplicate/overlapping geometry, or inconsistent face normals — common after exporting from CAD tools, mesh editors, or a buggy export step

Try

  • Most slicers (Cura, PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio) auto-repair small mesh errors on load — check if the preview actually looks solid despite the warning before worrying further
  • Run the file through a dedicated repair tool (Windows 3D Builder, Meshmixer, or Netfabb's online fixer) if slices come out visibly broken
  • Re-export the STL from the original source at a finer tolerance if repair doesn't help
